### DESIGN with purpose
Every design decision should have a clear purpose. Following Flusser's idea, design is strategic. It is usually intentional, with clear goals and outcomes in mind. It's not about aesthetics but about delivering meaning and function with intention.  

It's important to keep in mind that the purpose may reveal itself only after the finish line. A good example of design with purpose is creating a logo for a specific company or a product with a specific need. Yet there is also design needed for storytelling, where you don't always know where the story will go. Like the story, the design will progress. Meaningless attributes in a design often become a big piece of the puzzle, with the purpose revealed later.

---

### DESIGN as a solution
Good design exists to solve real problems. It focuses on creating solutions that make a change in people's lives. It isn’t about creativity or expression; it’s about addressing the needs and pain points of the users.  

As Flusser suggests, designers should be critical of the problems they are trying to solve. A design could look good in theory but lack the benefits that make a difference in people’s lives. Great design takes the real user’s perspective into account and creates solutions that work in practice.

---

### DESIGN your worth
Design with confidence, knowing that your creations will reflect your value. It’s a representation of yourself. What you send out into the world is how people will perceive not just "you," but your morals, ideas, work ethic, and skill.  

As the 37signals manifesto points out, design is a statement of who you are. Sloppy work sends a message of carelessness. Clients and users notice when a design is rushed or lacks thought. Every design should showcase your best effort, representing your worth as a designer. Your work should be a testament to your dedication, skill, and personal standards.

---

### DESIGN to improve
You never stop improving. As both Flusser and 37signals say, design is not a one-time act but a process of constant refinement.  

User feedback, testing, and actual use are essential for evolving any design. Expanding your styles and tastes is equally important. Designers evolve as their skills and knowledge grow, and their designs should reflect that growth. Design is an ongoing journey, constantly adapting and improving with new skills and perspectives.

---

### DESIGN for yourself
While designing for others, never lose sight of your creative vision. Stay true to what inspires and motivates you. Believe in what makes you create, and have fun with it, even if others find it silly or pointless.  

Flusser reminds us that design is playful—it's about making the world a little more engaging. A design that brings you joy or feels like a true expression of your creativity is reason enough to pursue it. The best designs come from passion, where function and personal expression intersect. If it makes you happy and it’s true to your vision, that’s what matters. Do it.

---

### DESIGN with (self-)care
Prioritize yourself and your health above everything. Set boundaries and maintain your joy.  

You cannot control everything that happens, but that doesn't mean it should take a toll on your health.
